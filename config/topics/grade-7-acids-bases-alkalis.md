## Definitions *(p. 40)*

In a school laboratory:

1. An **acid** may be defined as a substance that turns litmus red.
2. A **base** may be defined as a substance that turns litmus blue.

**Litmus** is a lichen found mainly in West Africa. It changes its colour depending on whether the solution it is in is basic/alkaline or acidic. It is thus able to identify/show whether another substance is an acid, base or neutral.

3. An **indicator** is a substance that shows whether another substance is a base/alkaline, acid or neutral.

## Mineral acids *(p. 41)*

The most commonly used acids found in a school laboratory are **not naturally occurring — they are manufactured**. They are called **mineral acids**.

| Name of mineral acid | Common use |
|---|---|
| Hydrochloric acid (HCl) | Used to clean/pickle surfaces of metals; is found in the stomach of mammals/human beings |
| Sulphuric(VI) acid (H₂SO₄) | Used as the acid in car batteries, making batteries, making fertilizers |
| Nitric(V) acid (HNO₃) | Used in making fertilizers and explosives |

Mineral acids are manufactured to very high concentration. They are **corrosive** (cause painful wounds on contact with the skin) and attack/react with garments/clothes/metals. In a school laboratory, they are mainly used after adding a lot of water — this is called **diluting**. Diluting ensures the concentration of the acid is safely low.

## Bases and alkalis *(p. 41)*

Bases are the opposite of acids. Most bases do not dissolve in water. **Bases which dissolve in water are called alkalis.**

### Common alkalis *(p. 41)*

| Name of alkali | Common use |
|---|---|
| Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) | Making soaps and detergents |
| Potassium hydroxide (KOH) | Making soaps and detergents |
| Ammonia solution (NH₄OH) | Making fertilizers, softening hard water |

### Common bases (which are not alkalis) *(p. 42)*

| Name of base | Common name/use |
|---|---|
| Magnesium oxide/hydroxide | Antacid to treat indigestion |
| Calcium oxide | Making cement and neutralizing soil acidity |
