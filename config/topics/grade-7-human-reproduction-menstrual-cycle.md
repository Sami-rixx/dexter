## What is ovulation? *(p. 47)*

When a young woman reaches puberty, she starts to ovulate. **Ovulation** is when a mature egg or ovum is released from one of the ovaries. The **ovaries** are the two female reproductive organs found in the pelvis.

- If the egg is fertilized by a sperm as it travels down the fallopian tube, then pregnancy occurs. The fertilized egg attaches to the lining of the uterus. The **placenta** then develops. The placenta transfers nutrition and oxygen to the fetus from the mother.
- If the egg does not become fertilized, the lining of the uterus (**endometrium**) is shed during menstruation.

The average menstrual cycle lasts **28 days**. The cycle starts with the first day of one period and ends with the first day of the next period. The average woman ovulates on **day 14**. At this time, some women have minor discomfort in their lower abdomen, spotting, or bleeding, while others do not have any symptoms at all.

A woman is generally most likely to get pregnant (fertile) if she has sex a few days before, and during, ovulation.

## The menstrual cycle *(p. 47)*

Each month during the years between puberty and menopause, a woman's body goes through a number of changes to get it ready for a possible pregnancy. This series of **hormone-driven events** is called the menstrual cycle.

During each menstrual cycle, an egg develops and is released from the ovaries. The lining of the uterus builds up. If a pregnancy doesn't happen, the uterine lining sheds during a menstrual period. Then the cycle starts again.

## The menstrual phase *(p. 47)*

The **menstrual phase** is the first stage of the menstrual cycle — it's also when a woman gets her period. This phase starts when an egg from the previous cycle isn't fertilized. Because pregnancy hasn't taken place, levels of the hormones **estrogen and progesterone** drop.

The thickened lining of the uterus, which would support a pregnancy, is no longer needed, so it sheds through the vagina. During the period, a woman releases a combination of blood, mucus, and tissue from the uterus.

### Period symptoms listed in the source *(pp. 47–48)*

- Cramps
- Tender breasts
- Bloating
- Mood swings
- Irritability
- Headaches
- Tiredness
- Low back pain

On average, women are in the menstrual phase of their cycle for **3 to 7 days**. Some women have longer periods than others. *(p. 48)*

## Identifying common issues related to the menstrual cycle *(p. 48)*

Every woman's menstrual cycle is different. Some women get their period at the same time each month; others are more irregular. Some women bleed more heavily or for a longer number of days than others. A menstrual cycle can also change during certain times of life — for example, it can get more irregular close to menopause.

One way to find out whether there are any issues with the menstrual cycle is to **track periods**: write down when they start and end, record any changes to the amount or number of days of bleeding, and whether there is spotting between periods.

### Things that can alter the menstrual cycle *(p. 48)*

- **Birth control** — the birth control pill may make periods shorter and lighter; while on some pills, no period occurs at all.
- **Pregnancy** — periods should stop during pregnancy; missed periods are one of the most obvious first signs of pregnancy.
- **Uterine fibroids** — these noncancerous growths in the uterus can make periods longer and heavier than usual.
- **Eating disorders** — anorexia, bulimia, and other eating disorders can disrupt the menstrual cycle and make periods stop.

### Signs of a problem with the menstrual cycle *(p. 48)*

- Skipped periods, or periods that have stopped entirely.
- Irregular periods.
- Bleeding for more than seven days.
- Periods less than 21 days or more than 35 days apart.
- Bleeding between periods (heavier than spotting).

If a person has these or other problems with their menstrual cycle or periods, they should talk to their healthcare provider. *(p. 49)*

## Managing issues related to the menstrual cycle *(p. 49)*

- Stopping smoking
- Using heating pads, hot water bottles, or warm baths to ease pain
- Trying gentle forms of exercise, such as walking
- Managing stress and making time for relaxation
- Avoiding caffeine, alcohol, and refined sugar in the 2 weeks before a period
- Getting enough sleep each night
